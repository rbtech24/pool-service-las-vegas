What dust actually does inside a pool system

Fine silt stays suspended until filtration catches it — or until it plates out on plaster and tile. Chlorine demand jumps because organics and particles consume oxidizer. Skimmer weirs choke. Pump strainers fill. Cartridge grids load from the outside in; sand beds channel if backwashed poorly; DE grids can bind. After a storm, the smartest first move is mechanical: baskets, then filtration, then chemistry — not random shock alone.

Step-by-step recovery we use on route

1) Empty skimmer and pump baskets. 2) Note filter PSI versus clean baseline. 3) Backwash or flag cartridge service. 4) Extend runtime for 24–48 hours. 5) Retest free chlorine, pH, and alkalinity. 6) Brush walls so dust does not stain. 7) Spot vacuum if a dust blanket settles. 8) Schedule filter deep cleaning if PSI stays high after the first recovery day. Preventive habits between storms

Keep baskets empty as a lifestyle. Do not run on a clogged filter “until next month.” Maintain residual chlorine so dust does not meet a dead oxidizer. Consider automatic cleaners as helpers, not replacements for weekly pool cleaning. After each major event, glance at the pressure gauge — it is the cheapest diagnostic instrument you own.
